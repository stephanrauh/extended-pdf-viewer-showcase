Since version 31.0.0-alpha.1, the bleeding-edge bundle offers three more editor buttons, contributed by legraina:

  • Eraser ([showEraserEditor], [disableEraserEditor]): erases parts of drawings and free-hand highlights. The slider next to the button sets the size of the eraser. Erasing can be undone, and it only affects drawings — text and text highlights stay untouched.
  • Undo and redo ([showUndoRedoButtons], [disableUndoRedoButtons]): undo and redo the last changes made with the annotation editor. With a keyboard, Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y (Cmd+Z and Cmd+Shift+Z on a Mac) do the same, so the buttons are mostly useful on tablets and phones. Like these shortcuts, they only work while an editor is active, so they're disabled otherwise, and also when there's nothing to undo or redo.

The toolbar is already full at 910 pixels, so both use the new responsive level 'xxxl' by default: they stay in the toolbar when it's at least 1000 pixels wide, and move to the secondary menu otherwise.

Note: the undo and redo buttons are shown by default during the 31.0.0 alphas only. From 31.0.0 on, they're hidden by default, so add [showUndoRedoButtons]="true" if your users work on touch devices. Keep the buttons in the toolbar rather than in the secondary menu — two taps per undo are hardly better than no button at all.

Try it: activate the Draw tool, draw something, then undo, redo, and erase it.
